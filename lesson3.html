Lesson 3: Differentiation and Applications

Understand derivatives, differentiation rules and how derivatives are used to analyze change and solve real-world problems.

1. Introduction to Differentiation

Differentiation is a fundamental concept in calculus used to measure the rate at which one quantity changes with respect to another.

If a function represents a changing quantity, its derivative tells us how rapidly that quantity is changing.

Example Consider:
f(x) = x²
Its derivative is:
f'(x) = 2x

2. Derivative of a Constant

The derivative of a constant is zero because a constant does not change as the variable changes.

d/dx(c) = 0
Example If:
f(x) = 8
Then:
f'(x) = 0

3. Power Rule

One of the most important differentiation rules is the power rule.

d/dx(xⁿ) = nxⁿ⁻¹
Example For:
f(x) = x⁵
Using the power rule:
f'(x) = 5x⁴

4. Derivative of a Polynomial

A polynomial can be differentiated term by term.

Example Consider:
f(x) = 3x³ + 2x² - 5x + 7
Differentiating each term:
f'(x) = 9x² + 4x - 5

5. Geometric Meaning of a Derivative

Geometrically, the derivative at a point represents the slope of the tangent line to the curve at that point.

A positive derivative indicates that the function is increasing, while a negative derivative indicates that the function is decreasing.

6. Applications of Differentiation

Differentiation has applications in many fields, including physics, engineering, economics, finance and computer science.

Example: Motion If the position of an object is:
s(t) = t² + 3t
Its velocity is the derivative:
v(t) = s'(t) = 2t + 3

7. Optimization

Derivatives can be used to find maximum and minimum values of functions. This is known as optimization.

Optimization is commonly used in business and engineering to minimize costs, maximize profit, improve efficiency and determine the best possible solution.

Quick Summary

Differentiation measures the rate of change of a function. Important rules include the constant rule and power rule. Derivatives also provide the slope of a curve and are widely used in motion, optimization, economics, engineering and other fields.
